# Focus area 5 — guideline-side attack/defense contribution

PROJECT_ID: `pitavastatin-ezetimibe-evidence` · RUN_ID: `2026-09-01-v1`
Owner: guideline-risk-intelligence · Wave 1

Scope: honest counter-arguments against a pro-pitavastatin/ezetimibe-FDC positioning, sourced from
this role's domain (guideline landscape), for the Director to integrate into `40_SYNTHESIS/`'s
attack/defense section alongside trials-efficacy's and safety-pharmacology's contributions. These
are genuine weak points, not straw men softened for effect — several of them are, in this role's
judgment, real enough that any manuscript/slide deck built on this project's evidence base should
address them directly rather than skip past them.

## Attack 1 — the 2026 ACC/AHA guideline does not endorse "early/upfront combination therapy"

Already established as a primary-source finding (`focus-area-1-guideline-wording.md` §2): the 2026
ACC/AHA guideline's structure is **stepwise**, not upfront-combination — Class 1 recommends statin
monotherapy first, with ezetimibe/PCSK9 mAb/bempedoic acid added only "on maximally tolerated statin
therapy" when the absolute goal is not yet met. **If this project's synthesis or manuscript asserts
that "current guidelines favor early combination therapy" as a general, transatlantic statement, that
overclaims for the US guideline specifically** — it is, at most, an ESC-specific position (itself
still not directly primary-verified by this project; see the prior run's own caveat on the Katzmann &
Laufs secondary-review corroboration). The defensible framing is narrower: *some* guideline bodies
(reportedly ESC 2025) have moved toward upfront combination in specific populations (treatment-naïve
ACS); the largest, most recently-verified US guideline has not.

**Defense available:** the 2026 ACC/AHA guideline did remove a real barrier to combination therapy —
the requirement that ezetimibe precede a PCSK9 mAb — and its Class 1 (not 2a) grading for ezetimibe/
PCSK9 add-on at very-high risk is a genuinely stronger endorsement of *eventual* combination than the
2018 guideline gave. "Faster path to combination, still statin-first" is accurate; "recommends
upfront combination" is not.

## Attack 2 — the guideline does not prefer ezetimibe over a PCSK9 mAb, though its structure is more graded than a flat three-way tie

**Corrected (Decision 2026-09-01-17)** — the original version of this attack over-generalized;
T-101's add-on structure is risk-stratified, not identically worded at every tier:

- **Not at very high risk (recommendations #2/#3, page ~e1207):** genuinely a three-way, equally
  graded choice — "ezetimibe, a PCSK9 mAb, or bempedoic acid (selection depending on degree of
  LDL-C lowering needed and patient preference)," all under the same COR 2a, LOE B-R.
- **At very high risk, the three options are NOT equally graded.** Recommendation #5 (page ~e1208,
  COR 1, LOE A) names only two: "ezetimibe and/or a PCSK9 mAb should be added (selected based on the
  degree of LDL-C lowering needed and patient preference)." Bempedoic acid is a **separate**
  recommendation (#6, COR 2a, LOE B-R: "reasonable to add bempedoic acid, with or without ezetimibe
  and/or PCSK9 mAb"), and inclisiran is a **third, separate, further-constrained** recommendation
  (#7, COR 2a, LOE B-R, explicitly limited to patients "unable to tolerate or obtain evolocumab or
  alirocumab or have a strong preference for less frequent dosing").

**The precise, defensible version of this attack**, narrower than originally stated: **the guideline
establishes no preference for ezetimibe over a PCSK9 mAb specifically** at very-high risk (both sit
at COR 1, LOE A within recommendation #5's shared "and/or," with the choice between them left to
"degree of LDL-C lowering needed and patient preference") — bempedoic acid and inclisiran are
genuinely lower-graded (COR 2a) and, for inclisiran, access-gated behind PCSK9 mAb intolerance/
unavailability. **This still fully supports the core point** — T-101 does not assert ezetimibe is
the guideline-preferred first non-statin add-on, that remains this project's own separate clinical
argument — but the honest limit of the claim is "no preference between ezetimibe and PCSK9 mAb
specifically" (very-high-risk tier) or "no preference among all three" (not-very-high-risk tier),
not a single blanket "all three/four options are always equal" statement.

**Sharpened version of this attack, specific to cost/access:** the guideline's own synopsis notes
that PCSK9 mAb "safety data have been reported, and the cost has decreased substantially" as the
stated rationale for *removing* the old ezetimibe-first sequencing rule. **This actively narrows one
of ezetimibe's traditional practical advantages** (cheap, oral, low-barrier step before an injectable,
costlier PCSK9 mAb) — in the current guideline landscape, a clinician has an explicit, guideline-
sanctioned option to go straight to a PCSK9 mAb or bempedoic acid instead of ezetimibe, on cost/
access grounds that are weaker than they used to be. Any "ezetimibe as the practical first choice"
argument in this project's synthesis should acknowledge this directly rather than treat ezetimibe's
practical-first-step status as an unchallenged given.

**Defense available:** ezetimibe remains the only oral, non-injectable, low-cost, decades-long-safety-
record option among the three — "guideline-neutral among three options" is not the same as
"clinically neutral"; the practical case for ezetimibe first can still be made, just not by citing
the guideline as taking that position for you.

## Attack 3 — nothing in T-101 addresses pitavastatin specifically, or the FDC product

T-101 is statin-agnostic throughout every recommendation this role extracted — "maximally tolerated
statin," never a named statin. **Citing 2026 ACC/AHA's COR 1 ezetimibe-add-on recommendation as
support for the pitavastatin/ezetimibe FDC specifically is a conflation risk this project must not
make** — it is exactly the same category of error this project's own governance documents (`CLAUDE.md`
§7, the prior run's Wave 2 challenge round) already flag for RACING (rosuvastatin, not pitavastatin)
and REPRIEVE/REAL-CAD (pitavastatin monotherapy, not the FDC). T-101 supports "a statin + ezetimibe
strategy is guideline-endorsed"; it says nothing about *which* statin, and nothing about a fixed-dose
combination product versus separate pills. The FDC-specific efficacy/adherence case has to be made
from trials-efficacy's own evidence base (Phase III FDC trial, Katzmann/Samnaliev-type adherence
data), not borrowed from T-101's authority.

## Attack 4 — the Taiwan STS "ezetimibe as first add-on" claim, this project's own central framing device, remains unverified against primary text

Carried forward and re-confirmed this Wave (`blocked-sources-recheck.md`): the Taiwan STS 2026
consensus itself — the document `pitavastatin topic.md` leans on most heavily for this project's
"goal-directed, STS-aware" reframing — is still `BLOCKED_FOR_SOURCE`. Everything this project has
said about its content traces to the PI's own paraphrase, not independently verified primary text.
**This is, in this role's assessment (consistent with the prior run's own Wave 2 challenge round),
the single largest unverified-but-load-bearing claim in this project's overall framing** — more
consequential than any individual trial-level gap, because it is the organizing concept the whole
"treat the target, not the dose" narrative is built around. A rigorous attack/defense section should
say this plainly rather than let a well-supported 2026-ACC/AHA finding (Attacks 1–3 above) create a
false sense that the *Taiwan-specific* STS claim has been equally well-verified — it has not.

## Attack 5 — the "maximize statin first" counter-position has genuine 2026-guideline backing, not just historical inertia

Focus area 5's own framing names "'maximize statin first' counter-position" as an attack to address.
**This is not merely a legacy position this project needs to argue against — it is, in a real sense,
what the newly-verified 2026 ACC/AHA guideline itself still recommends as the first step** (Attack 1).
A defensible synthesis should present this honestly: the debate is not "guidelines have moved past
statin-maximization to combination-first" (they mostly have not, per the largest US guideline) — it
is "how quickly should a clinician move from statin-maximization to combination, and does a
pitavastatin/ezetimibe FDC make that transition easier without giving up meaningful LDL-C-lowering
efficacy compared to further statin dose escalation." That is a narrower, more defensible question
than "combination is now the guideline standard."

## Summary for `40_SYNTHESIS/`

The strongest, most defensible version of this project's guideline-based case is: (1) 2026 ACC/AHA
has reintroduced absolute goal-directed targets and removed a real barrier to combination therapy
(ezetimibe-before-PCSK9i sequencing), genuinely supporting *earlier and easier* combination than 2018
— but not "upfront" combination, and not ezetimibe-preferred among nonstatin options; (2) none of
this guideline evidence is pitavastatin-specific or FDC-specific — that case rests entirely on
trials-efficacy's own evidence base; (3) the Taiwan-specific "ezetimibe as first add-on" framing this
project's own topic notes lean on most heavily remains unverified against primary text and should be
flagged as such wherever it appears, not smoothed into the same confidence tier as the now-verified
T-101 findings.
