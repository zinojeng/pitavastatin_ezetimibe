# Pitavastatin/Ezetimibe Fixed-Dose Combination (FDC): Clinical Evidence Review

**An evidence overview of pitavastatin, ezetimibe, and the pitavastatin/ezetimibe fixed-dose combination in the management of dyslipidemia**

*English version — companion to the Traditional Chinese original, `pitavastatin-ezetimibe-final-report.md`. Content, numbers, and citations are identical; this is a direct translation, not a re-derivation.*

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The pitavastatin/ezetimibe fixed-dose combination (FDC) has solid, internationally reproduced direct evidence on the surrogate endpoint of LDL-C lowering: a Phase III trial conducted in Taiwan/Australia/New Zealand (Chou et al., *Clin Ther* 2022) and the Japanese K-924 trial (Tsujita et al., *J Atheroscler Thromb* 2023) both consistently show that adding ezetimibe 10 mg produces a statistically significant additional LDL-C reduction on top of pitavastatin monotherapy. Pitavastatin's own glycemic profile and low CYP3A4-dependent interaction liability also support a reasonable positioning case in elderly, polypharmacy, and East Asian populations.

However, to be honest, the FDC **itself** does not yet have statistically significant hard cardiovascular-outcome superiority evidence — the only randomized controlled trial that directly tests pitavastatin+ezetimibe vs. pitavastatin monotherapy for cardiovascular events (HIJ-PROPER) did not reach statistical significance on its overall primary endpoint (HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.76–1.04, P=0.152). In addition, the Taiwan STS 2026 consensus — which this review's own "ezetimibe as the preferred first-line Taiwan add-on" framing leans on most heavily — has a specific content claim that has, to date, **not been independently verified in full text**. This is the single highest-risk item among all supporting claims in this review, and must be disclosed clearly to the reader rather than placed at the same confidence tier as verified international guideline content. The purpose of this document is to organize the already-verified evidence into a clinically usable reading document while preserving these honest limitations.

## 2. Three-Level Hard-Outcome Evidence Hierarchy

The hard cardiovascular-outcome evidence for pitavastatin/ezetimibe FDC must be understood as three independent, mutually non-substitutable levels:

| Level | Content | Current status |
|---|---|---|
| **Level 1 — LDL-lowering efficacy** | The FDC's own LDL-C lowering evidence | **Strong, product-level, internationally reproduced** — consistent results from the Taiwan/Australia/NZ Phase III trial and the Japanese K-924 trial. `DIRECT EVIDENCE` / LDL-C surrogate efficacy. |
| **Level 2 — Combination-strategy CV outcome evidence** | Hard-outcome evidence for the "statin + ezetimibe" combination strategy itself | **Strong, but not pitavastatin-specific** — from RACING (rosuvastatin 10 mg + ezetimibe 10 mg vs. high-intensity monotherapy, 3-year MACE 9.1% vs. 9.9%, non-inferior). `INDIRECT EVIDENCE` (a different statin, must not be cited as pitavastatin-specific evidence). The 2026 ACC/AHA guideline's formal recommendation for adding ezetimibe also belongs to this level: statin-agnostic `GUIDELINE/CONSENSUS`. |
| **Level 3 — Pitavastatin/ezetimibe-specific hard-outcome superiority** | Superiority of the FDC itself for cardiovascular events | **Not yet directly or sufficiently demonstrated.** HIJ-PROPER's overall primary endpoint (HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.76–1.04, P=0.152) did not reach statistical significance and remains the only RCT that directly tests this question. Two subgroup analyses (see Section 4) are hypothesis-generating only and do not substitute for superiority evidence. |

No presentation or argument should sum the evidentiary strength of Levels 1 + 2 + 3 to imply that, together, they amount to proven hard-outcome superiority for the FDC itself — this is a logical fallacy of adding evidence strengths across different levels.

## 3. 2026 ACC/AHA Guideline Positioning

### 3.1 What this document is, and how it was verified

The 2026 ACC/AHA/AACVPR/ABC/ACPM/ADA/AGS/APhA/ASPC/NLA/PCNA multi-society dyslipidemia management guideline (*Circulation*. 2026;153:e1154–e1276, DOI `10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423`) is this review's newly introduced and most important primary source. Its authenticity was confirmed through three independent, externally verifiable methods: (1) DOI resolution genuinely redirects to the official *Circulation* publisher article page; (2) the Crossref public API's title, journal, volume, publisher, and author list, plus an independently cross-checked publication date, all match the document's own content exactly; (3) a systematic (not sampled) internal-coherence review across all 123 pages, covering unit conversions, COR/LOE formatting, and per-page watermarks.

**An honest verification limitation that must be disclosed**: the publisher's website itself presents an access barrier to this project's automated tools, so the verification above is **verification by independent corroboration** (DOI resolution + Crossref metadata + systematic internal-coherence review), not a byte-for-byte re-download comparison against the publisher's official file. This does not diminish the validity of the three independent checks themselves, but readers should know this is an honest limitation in verification method — not "an officially confirmed copy obtained from the publisher."

### 3.2 Goal-Directed Therapy

This guideline structures its entire treatment framework around **absolute LDL-C/non-HDL-C targets** (not merely relative percentage reductions), with different targets set by risk stratum:

| Risk stratum | LDL-C target | non-HDL-C target | COR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diabetes, no ASCVD, standard risk | <100 mg/dL (2.6 mmol/L) | <130 mg/dL (3.4 mmol/L) | 1 |
| Diabetes, multiple ASCVD risk factors | <70 mg/dL (1.8 mmol/L) | <100 mg/dL (2.6 mmol/L) | 2a |
| Clinical ASCVD, not very high risk | <70 mg/dL (1.8 mmol/L) | <100 mg/dL | 1 |
| **Clinical ASCVD, very high risk (VHR)** | **<55 mg/dL (1.4 mmol/L)** | **<85 mg/dL (2.2 mmol/L)** | **1, LOE A** |
| CAC ≥1000 AU | <55 mg/dL (1.4 mmol/L) | <85 mg/dL (2.2 mmol/L) | 1 |

**Three-tier structure for severe hypercholesterolemia (LDL-C ≥190 mg/dL)**:

| Tier | LDL-C target | non-HDL-C target | COR/LOE |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1: no ASCVD/HeFH/subclinical atherosclerosis/other risk factors | <100 mg/dL (2.6 mmol/L) | <130 mg/dL (3.4 mmol/L) | 1, B-NR |
| Tier 2: with HeFH/subclinical atherosclerosis/other risk factors, no clinical ASCVD | <70 mg/dL (1.8 mmol/L) | <100 mg/dL (**2.6 mmol/L**)† | 1, B-R |
| Tier 3: with clinical ASCVD | <55 mg/dL (1.4 mmol/L) | <85 mg/dL (2.2 mmol/L) | 1, B-R |

† **A printing inconsistency in the source that must be disclosed to the reader**: the guideline's own authoritative numbered-recommendation section (page e1199) prints the Tier 2 non-HDL-C target as <100 mg/dL **(2.6 mmol/L)**, which matches the standard unit conversion for 100 mg/dL and is consistent with every other appearance of "<100 mg/dL" throughout the document — this document adopts 2.6 mmol/L on that basis. However, the same guideline's own "Table 1. 2018 vs 2026" summary comparison table (page e1159) prints the same target as **2.4 mmol/L** — this is a printing inconsistency **internal to the source publication itself** (most plausibly a typo in the summary table), not an artifact of any extraction or translation process in this review, and is therefore retained as an annotation rather than dropped. The LDL-C target itself (<70 mg/dL) is consistent at both locations and unaffected.

### 3.3 Ezetimibe Add-On Positioning: VHR and Non-VHR Are Graded Differently — Must Be Stated Separately

The guideline's formal recommendations for adding a non-statin drug on top of a statin (Secondary ASCVD Prevention chapter) have **materially different recommendation strengths by risk stratum, and must not be stated as one blanket claim**:

**Non-VHR (COR 2a, LOE B-R, genuinely equal three-way)**: it is reasonable to add ezetimibe, a PCSK9 mAb, or bempedoic acid, with the choice depending on the degree of LDL-C lowering needed and patient preference — at this tier, **there genuinely is no guideline preference among the three**.

**VHR (very high risk) — NOT equal, must be listed separately**:
- **Ezetimibe and/or PCSK9 mAb (COR 1, LOE A)**: "should be added" (stronger wording), with the choice between the two based on degree of lowering needed and patient preference — **no preference between these two specifically**.
- **Bempedoic acid (COR 2a, LOE B-R, a separate, weaker recommendation)**: may be added with or without ezetimibe/PCSK9 mAb.
- **Inclisiran (COR 2a, LOE B-R, a separate recommendation with explicit use constraints)**: explicitly limited to patients "unable to tolerate or obtain" PCSK9 mAb, or with a strong preference for less-frequent dosing — not a general equal option.

**The precise, non-simplifiable conclusion**: at the VHR tier, the guideline genuinely has no preference for "ezetimibe over PCSK9 mAb" (both listed at COR 1, LOE A); but it does have a formal preference for "ezetimibe/PCSK9 mAb over bempedoic acid/inclisiran" (COR 1 vs. COR 2a). The correct one-sentence statement is: *"At VHR, there is no guideline preference for ezetimibe over PCSK9 mAb specifically, but the guideline does formally favor ezetimibe/PCSK9 mAb over bempedoic acid/inclisiran at this tier; at the non-VHR tier, all three are genuinely equal."* This must not be simplified to "all four options are equal at every risk tier."

**Two negative findings that must not be conflated**: (1) this guideline does **not** use "upfront combination therapy" as formal recommendation language — its structure remains **stepwise**: statin monotherapy at the maximally tolerated dose first, with add-on only if the target is not met. Upfront combination is not the formal position of the largest, currently verified US guideline; if the 2025 ESC/EAS position is cited as supporting evidence, it must be explicitly labeled as a European-specific position, not written as a transatlantic consensus. (2) The guideline did genuinely **remove an existing sequencing constraint** — it no longer requires ezetimibe to be added before initiating a PCSK9 mAb — but "removing a sequencing constraint" and "recommending upfront combination" are two conceptually different things.

**A class-level statement about East Asian populations**: the guideline explicitly states that "East Asian ancestry may be more prone to side effects due to inherited drug metabolism effects, thus initial treatment should be with lower doses" — this is a general **statin-class** statement, does not name pitavastatin specifically, and must not be rewritten as "the guideline recommends pitavastatin as preferred for East Asian patients."

## 4. Direct Pitavastatin+Ezetimibe Evidence

### 4.1 Phase III/FDC Trial (Chou et al., *Clin Ther* 2022)

A multinational, multicenter trial in Taiwan, Australia, and New Zealand comparing pitavastatin 2 mg/ezetimibe 10 mg FDC vs. pitavastatin 2 mg monotherapy vs. ezetimibe 10 mg monotherapy: LDL-C reduction at week 4 was −51.04% (FDC) vs. −34.99% (pitavastatin) vs. −20.01% (ezetimibe); at week 12, −50.5% (FDC) vs. −36.11% (pitavastatin) vs. −19.85% (ezetimibe) — the FDC reached statistical significance against each monotherapy component at both week 4 and week 12 (each p<0.001). Safety profiles were broadly comparable across the three arms. `DIRECT EVIDENCE` / LDL-C surrogate efficacy — this is LDL-C lowering evidence for the FDC itself, not cardiovascular hard-outcome evidence.

### 4.2 K-924 Dose Comparison (Japan, Tsujita et al., *J Atheroscler Thromb* 2023, DOI 10.5551/jat.64006)

The K-924 trial used an MMRM statistical model to compare the FDC vs. monotherapy at matched doses, yielding the following least-squares mean differences, independently verified against the primary full text:

- **FDC 2 mg/ezetimibe 10 mg vs. pitavastatin 2 mg monotherapy**: −11.9% (95% CI −15.2 to −8.7)
- **FDC 4 mg/ezetimibe 10 mg vs. pitavastatin 4 mg monotherapy**: −12.7% (95% CI −15.9 to −9.4)

These are the trial's genuinely prespecified, formally statistically tested results for "the additional effect of adding ezetimibe."

**An incorrect comparison that must be explicitly retracted and no longer cited**: a previously circulated comparison — "pitavastatin 2 mg+ezetimibe 10 mg (−51.4%) outperforms pitavastatin 4 mg monotherapy (−45.2%)" — was, after verification, confirmed to **never have been** a prespecified, statistically tested contrast in this trial. These two numbers are simply the descriptive point-estimate percentages of two different treatment arms subtracted from each other, not a formal head-to-head statistical comparison. No presentation or document should cite "−51.4% vs. −45.2%" under an "adding ezetimibe beats dose-doubling" framing again — the verified matched-dose LS-mean differences above should be cited instead.

### 4.3 HIJ-PROPER and Subgroup Analyses

HIJ-PROPER (Hagiwara et al., *Eur Heart J* 2017) is the only RCT that directly tests the effect of pitavastatin+ezetimibe vs. pitavastatin monotherapy on cardiovascular hard outcomes. Overall primary endpoint: 32.8% vs. 36.9%, **HR 0.89 (95% CI 0.76–1.04, P=0.152) — did not reach statistical superiority**.

Two subgroup analyses provide hypothesis-generating signals, but they are **conceptually independent of each other, must not be conflated, and must not be upgraded** to a proven superiority claim:

- **Sitosterol/cholesterol-absorber-phenotype subgroup** (Yamaguchi et al., *Atherosclerosis* 2018;274:139-145): among the subgroup with higher baseline serum sitosterol, HR 0.71 (95% CI 0.56–0.91).
- **Baseline-LDL-C-stratified subgroup** (*Scientific Reports* 2021, a statin-naive subset, N=1429, stratified at the median of 131 mg/dL): low-LDL-C group (N=686) HR 1.13 (95% CI 0.87–1.47, P=0.35, no significant difference); **high-LDL-C group (N=743) HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.56–0.91, P=0.007)**; **interaction P=0.012**, supporting that baseline LDL-C is genuinely an effect modifier rather than a coincidental signal from an arbitrary cut point.

Together, these two subgroup signals point toward the general direction that "not all patients benefit equally from adding ezetimibe" — a reasonable, open question in "precision lipidology" — but they **must be stated separately, each clearly labeled by which variable defines the stratification**, and neither can be used to claim "pitavastatin+ezetimibe has been proven to have cardiovascular benefit in a specific patient subgroup" — the parent trial's overall primary endpoint still did not reach significance.

## 5. Clinical Positioning

### 5.1 vs. Pitavastatin Monotherapy

The core argument is not about "replacing" monotherapy, but about whether, for patients not yet at their LDL-C target on pitavastatin 2 mg (or even after up-titration to 4 mg), adding ezetimibe 10 mg can provide a meaningful additional reduction — the verified figures in Section 4 support this. This should be considered together with pitavastatin's known low CYP3A4-dependent interaction liability and relatively favorable glycemic profile (see Section 6), together constituting an add-on option that balances efficacy, tolerability, and polypharmacy risk — rather than a simple "more potent" marketing claim.

### 5.2 vs. High-Intensity Statin Monotherapy

**The largest direct evidence gap that must be honestly disclosed**: there is currently **no** direct head-to-head trial of pitavastatin/ezetimibe FDC vs. high-intensity statin monotherapy (high-dose atorvastatin/rosuvastatin). The available indirect evidence each has its own limitations:

- **RACING** (rosuvastatin 10 mg + ezetimibe 10 mg vs. high-intensity monotherapy, 3-year MACE 9.1% vs. 9.9%, non-inferior; diabetes subgroup MACE 10.0% vs. 11.3%, not significant; intolerance-related discontinuation/dose reduction 4.8% vs. 8.2%, diabetes subgroup 5.2% vs. 8.7%) provides a tolerability-advantage signal for the **moderate-intensity statin + ezetimibe strategy** relative to high-intensity monotherapy, but this is the rosuvastatin version and cannot be directly extrapolated to pitavastatin.
- **K-924** provides an internal pitavastatin dose comparison, and is likewise not a direct comparison of "pitavastatin+eze vs. high-intensity atorvastatin/rosuvastatin."

On the other side, the opposing position — "maximize the statin dose first, then consider adding" — also has direct evidence support of its own, and **is not a position that needs to be argued away as outdated**: **REAL-CAD** (pitavastatin 4 mg vs. 1 mg, a Japanese secondary-prevention coronary artery disease population, HR 0.81, 95% CI 0.69–0.95, P=0.01) is a positive cardiovascular hard-outcome RCT for the pitavastatin dose-intensification strategy itself, with a level of evidence **more complete** than the FDC's current Level 3 evidence, which reaches only the subgroup level; and the 2026 ACC/AHA guideline itself also uses "statin first, add-on only after maximally tolerated" as its formal, stepwise recommendation structure. The honest, defensible framing of the question should be: "when a patient can tolerate dose escalation, can the FDC make the transition faster and more comfortable without sacrificing a meaningful LDL-C reduction" — the FDC's advantage lies mainly in Level 1 (greater reduction) and indirect tolerability/adherence signals, not in the stronger, currently unsupported claim that "the FDC itself has been proven to reduce cardiovascular events more than dose maximization."

### 5.3 vs. Other Nonstatin Add-Ons

| Drug class | Primary cardiovascular hard-outcome trial | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| Bempedoic acid | CLEAR-Outcomes (Nissen et al., *NEJM* 2023, DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2215024) | A completed, published positive CV outcome RCT (in a high-risk, statin-intolerant population) |
| PCSK9 mAb (evolocumab) | FOURIER (*NEJM* 2017, DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa1615664) | A completed, published positive CV outcome RCT |
| PCSK9 mAb (alirocumab) | ODYSSEY OUTCOMES (*NEJM* 2018, DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa1801174) | A completed, published positive CV outcome RCT (post-ACS population) |
| **Inclisiran** | VICTORION-2 Prevent, ORION-4 (design/protocol papers only) | **No completed, published cardiovascular hard-outcome results paper was found within this review's search scope** — only LDL-C surrogate evidence is adequate |

**An asymmetry that must be honestly disclosed**: the argument "rather than using pitavastatin+ezetimibe FDC, whose Level 3 evidence is weak, one should go directly to a hard-outcome-proven newer agent" **holds up** for bempedoic acid and PCSK9-targeting therapy (both have an evidence level superior to the FDC's current subgroup-only evidence); but it **does not hold up for inclisiran** — inclisiran itself does not yet have published cardiovascular hard-outcome results, only adequate LDL-C surrogate evidence and ongoing trial designs. Any "hard-outcome-proven alternative" framing that groups inclisiran with bempedoic acid/PCSK9 mAb at the same tier without this caveat constitutes an overextension.

When safety/tolerability grounds (rather than pure efficacy grounds) more strongly support an alternative: (1) established statin intolerance (the FDC still contains a statin component and theoretically could still trigger the same mechanism of muscle-related adverse effects; in this case, ezetimibe monotherapy + bempedoic acid, or PCSK9-targeting therapy, is more mechanistically conservative); (2) known high-risk OATP1B1-related polypharmacy (post-transplant cyclosporine, concomitant gemfibrozil); (3) patients on maintenance dialysis (ESRD/HD) (pitavastatin monotherapy dosing guidance is clear, but FDC-specific safety data in this population is entirely lacking). All three points above are at the level of mechanistic inference, not direct comparative-trial evidence, and should be labeled in a presentation as "a conservative option given a data gap," not "proven to be safer."

## 6. Four Safety Pillars

### 6.1 Glycemic Profile

Pitavastatin, relative to other commonly used high-potency statins, **may have a more favorable glycemic profile** — this is the only calibrated phrasing that should be used, **never** "does not cause diabetes" or "pitavastatin is the safe first choice for patients with diabetes." Supporting literature includes meta-analyses (relative risk RR 0.86 vs. atorvastatin, RR 0.77 vs. rosuvastatin) and real-world cohort studies, but these are all **relative** risks, not evidence of "no effect." An honest counter-point that must be proactively disclosed: high-dose data (CAPITAIN) still shows a slight increase in fasting plasma glucose (+4%, p<0.05) — high-dose therapy is not completely glucose-neutral, though the clinical significance is minor and should not be denied. There is also currently no pitavastatin-specific head-to-head dose-escalation RCT with a glycemic endpoint — the dose-response argument is stitched together across trials, methodologically weaker than a genuine dose-ranging design.

### 6.2 Drug-Drug Interactions (DDI)

The correct phrasing is "pitavastatin has low CYP3A4-dependent interaction liability, reducing the risk of interaction with CYP3A4 inhibitors" — **never** simplified to "low DDI" or "no DDI." The gap most easily overlooked, and most in need of proactive disclosure, is this: **low CYP3A4 dependence does not mean low OATP1B1 dependence**. OATP1B1 is pitavastatin's primary clearance mechanism, and interactions through this pathway still exist — cyclosporine is contraindicated for concomitant use, and gemfibrozil is recommended to be avoided; neither risk is exempted by "low CYP3A4 dependence." The interaction figures listed on the FDA label reflect known, tested combinations, and do not mean that all possible interactions have been ruled out.

### 6.3 CKD

Pitavastatin monotherapy's dosing ceiling in moderate-to-severe CKD (eGFR 15–59) or dialysis patients (1 mg starting dose, 2 mg maximum) is the most conservative among all listed statins. **However, FDC-specific dosing/safety data in the CKD population is entirely lacking** — this remains an unresolved gap, and monotherapy-component data cannot substitute for FDC-specific data.

### 6.4 Adherence

The FDC's adherence advantage relative to the equivalent free-drug combination (separate-pill combination) has been consistently observed across multiple observational studies (e.g., FDC LDL-C reduction of −28.4% vs. −19.4% for separate dosing, p<0.0001), but these studies **lack a direct measurement of adherence itself** — the reduction difference favoring the FDC over separate dosing is derived through an inference chain ("adherence improvement" is a reasonable explanation), not a directly demonstrated causal mechanism; this must be honestly disclosed as inference rather than direct evidence. Other studies show that the overall cohort did not observe a significant association between persistence/adherence and cardiovascular events (a signal was observed only in a specific dose subgroup) — this inconsistency must also be proactively disclosed, and one must not selectively cite only the subgroup result favorable to the argument. **Real-world evidence specific to pitavastatin/ezetimibe FDC currently consists of only one Taiwanese, two-center, single-arm, N=120 post-PCI cohort study** — the evidence base remains preliminary; real-world evidence for a similar but non-pitavastatin FDC (e.g., atorvastatin/ezetimibe) is accumulating for other drugs, and this asymmetry should be honestly presented, not framed as if the pitavastatin FDC already has adequate real-world evidence support.

## 7. Remaining Limitations and Gaps

**Taiwan STS 2026 consensus — this review's largest unverified-but-load-bearing claim**: the bibliographic information (authors, journal, DOI) for the Taiwan STS (Suboptimally Tolerable Statins) 2026 consensus has been confirmed to exist, and its publication licensing status has also been confirmed as genuine Gold Open Access/CC BY. However, its **specific content claim** — "repositioning ezetimibe as the preferred first-line add-on for high/very-high-risk patients" — **still cannot be independently verified in full text** to date; the publisher's page remains inaccessible through any lawful channel available to this review. This is, in this review's overall "goal-directed rather than dose-directed" framework, the **single largest unverified-but-load-bearing claim** — more consequential than any single trial-level evidence gap, because it is the organizing concept the entire "treat the target, not the dose" narrative depends on. **The verified content of the US 2026 ACC/AHA guideline cannot and should not be used to indirectly substantiate or obscure this Taiwan-specific gap — the two are different documents, in different jurisdictional contexts.** Any occasion citing "Taiwan STS repositions ezetimibe as the preferred add-on" must explicitly note that this is bibliographically verified but content-unverified, and must not be presented at the same confidence tier as the verified T-101 guideline content.

Other honest, unresolved gaps:

- **No direct pitavastatin/ezetimibe FDC vs. high-intensity statin monotherapy head-to-head trial exists** — this is the largest direct evidence gap in the safety/tolerability positioning argument (see Section 5.2 for detail).
- **FDC-specific CKD dosing safety data is entirely lacking** — only monotherapy-component dosing guidance currently exists; the FDC itself is not covered.
- **Real-world evidence for pitavastatin+ezetimibe FDC currently consists of only one small, single-arm, two-center study** (Taiwan, N=120, post-PCI cohort).
- **Inclisiran has no published cardiovascular hard-outcome trial** — only design/protocol papers exist; it should not be grouped with bempedoic acid or PCSK9-targeting therapy as an equal-tier "hard-outcome-proven" alternative.
- **The two HIJ-PROPER subgroup analyses have not yet been independently full-text verified** (the quantitative content of one is currently sourced only from an aggregated abstract; the other's HR/CI/p-values carry only moderate confidence, not cross-verified through a second channel) — these confidence-level qualifiers should be retained when cited, and the numbers should not be treated as fully confirmed.
- **Taiwan National Health Insurance coverage and co-payment information for this FDC was not investigated in this review** — a localization information gap that should be separately confirmed before real-world clinical/educational application.

## 8. Conclusion: What Can and Cannot Be Said

**Can be said:**

- Pitavastatin/ezetimibe FDC, relative to pitavastatin monotherapy, provides a statistically significant additional LDL-C reduction (consistently supported by the Phase III trial and the K-924 trial), and this has been internationally reproduced.
- The 2026 ACC/AHA guideline supports goal-directed therapy, with a VHR-population LDL-C target of <55 mg/dL and non-HDL-C target of <85 mg/dL, and gives a strong COR 1, LOE A recommendation for adding ezetimibe and/or a PCSK9 mAb, with no preference between the two.
- Pitavastatin, relative to other commonly used high-potency statins, may have a more favorable glycemic profile, and has low CYP3A4 dependence, reducing the risk of interaction with CYP3A4 inhibitors.
- The two HIJ-PROPER subgroup analyses (cholesterol-absorber phenotype, baseline-LDL-C stratification) provide a reasonable, worth-tracking hypothesis for future precision lipidology.
- REAL-CAD provides statistically significant, positive cardiovascular hard-outcome evidence for the pitavastatin monotherapy dose-intensification strategy itself.

**Cannot be said:**

- Cannot say pitavastatin/ezetimibe FDC itself has been proven to reduce cardiovascular events — HIJ-PROPER's overall primary endpoint did not reach statistical significance, and this is the only RCT that directly tests this question.
- Cannot cite RACING, REAL-CAD, or the 2026 ACC/AHA guideline content as pitavastatin/ezetimibe FDC-specific cardiovascular hard-outcome evidence — the first two studied different populations (a different statin, or no ezetimibe component), and the guideline is a statin-agnostic, general recommendation.
- Cannot say "the guideline recommends that patients at every risk tier be given ezetimibe preferentially over PCSK9 mAb, bempedoic acid, or inclisiran" — at the VHR tier, only ezetimibe and PCSK9 mAb have no preference between them, while bempedoic acid/inclisiran are formally disfavored relative to those two; only at the non-VHR tier are all three genuinely equal.
- Cannot say pitavastatin "does not cause diabetes" or has "no DDI" — the correct phrasings are, respectively, "may have a more favorable glycemic profile" and "low CYP3A4-dependent interaction liability," and both a slight glucose-elevation signal at high doses and the ongoing OATP1B1-pathway risk (cyclosporine, gemfibrozil) must be disclosed at the same time.
- Cannot group inclisiran with bempedoic acid or PCSK9-targeting therapy as an equal-tier "hard-outcome-proven" alternative — inclisiran currently has no published cardiovascular hard-outcome trial.
- Cannot state the two HIJ-PROPER subgroup signals as a single combined conclusion, or upgrade either one to a proven superiority claim.
- Cannot cite the Taiwan STS 2026 consensus's specific content claim ("ezetimibe as the preferred add-on") as a fact with the same certainty as a verified international guideline — its bibliographic information has been verified, but the content itself has not yet been independently confirmed in full text.
